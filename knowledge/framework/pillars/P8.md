# P8 · Team & Ops

P8  ·  Team, Operations & Resilience  ·  Family E: Operational, Infrastructure & Governance
Vault-type applicability note: Applies universally. Key person risk and monitoring critical for all vault types.
ID | Category | Criterion | What to Measure / How to Verify | Status | Institutional Standard | RF / Critical Conditions | Failure Case Anchor
8.1 | Team Quality | Team Credentials, Experience & Verifiability | Are founders and key staff doxxed and verifiable? Relevant experience (finance + crypto)? Prior successful exits or scaled products? Anonymous team? | Gap with action | Doxxed founding team. ≥5yr relevant experience. Verifiable prior track record. Tier-1 VC backing or institutional backing signals diligence done. | RF05 · Fully anonymous team; <1yr experience; no verifiable track record; previous failures undisclosed | Multiple DeFi exploits and rug pulls: anonymous teams with no track record exited or were unable to respond competently to crises
8.2 | Key Person Risk | Bus Factor & Succession Planning | If the CEO or lead developer was hit by a bus tomorrow, could the protocol continue operating? How many people have critical access? Is knowledge documented? | Gap with action | ≥10 person team. No single key person with irreplaceable access. Knowledge documented. Backup signers for multisig. Succession plan exists. | Single founder with all critical access; team <3 people; no documentation; backup signer absent; protocol dies if one person is unavailable | Multiple protocols: founder's sudden departure or unavailability made it impossible to respond to exploits or governance crises
8.3 | Real-Time Monitoring | Dashboard, Data Lag & Alert Infrastructure | Is there a live dashboard? What is the data lag for key risk metrics? Who receives alerts? What is the incident response SLA? | Gap with action | <5min data lag. Public dashboard (Dune/Nansen). Real-time alerts for risk breaches. <15min incident response SLA. Performance attribution live. | RF15 · No dashboard; data lag >24hrs; manual monitoring only; no alert system; response time undocumented | Euler Finance: real-time monitoring gaps meant exploit ran for several minutes before detection, significantly increasing losses
8.4 | Reporting | Institutional-Grade Reporting & Audit Trail | Daily P&L, monthly statements, quarterly audited reports? Who is the auditor? Is there a complete transaction audit trail? | Gap with action | Daily P&L; monthly statements; quarterly audit by Tier-1 crypto audit firm; complete on-chain transaction history; reports delivered automatically. | No regular reporting; no audited statements; no audit trail; reports delivered manually on request only | Institutional allocators could not satisfy their own LPs about position values because crypto managers had no institutional-grade reporting
8.5 | Infrastructure Resilience | Disaster Recovery, Multi-Region & Continuity | Multi-region infrastructure? DR plan tested when? Runbooks documented? RTO/RPO defined? Are smart contract signer keys geographically distributed? | Gap with action | Multi-region infrastructure. DR plan tested annually. <1hr RTO. Documented runbooks. Keys geographically distributed. Single points of failure eliminated. | Single region; no DR plan; RTO undefined; no runbooks; keys concentrated in one location; untested backup systems | Multiple exchanges: single data centre failures and poorly documented procedures amplified operational crises into customer-facing outages
8.6 | Governance | Protocol Governance Quality & Manipulation Resistance | On-chain or off-chain governance? Token distribution (Gini coefficient)? Participation rate? Timelock? Can a whale pass malicious proposals? | Gap with action | On-chain governance with ≥48hr timelock. Token Gini <0.75. ≥10% participation on material proposals. Emergency multisig separate from governance. | No on-chain governance; Gini >0.85 (whale controls); <5% participation; no emergency pause; vote buying / flash loan governance attack possible | Beanstalk: 79% of governance tokens acquired via flash loan in single block; no timelock meant malicious proposal passed instantly
8.7 | Contributor Vetting | Third-Party Contributor Vetting & Social Engineering Exposure | Assess the protocol's exposure to compromise through third-party contributors, contractors, and external collaborators. Mandatory questions: (1) What vetting process applies to third-party contributors who receive access to repositories, development tools, or internal systems? (2) Do multisig signers have documented identity verification beyond their public wallet address? Are signers required to use hardware wallets? (3) Is there a documented policy for code review and approval before third-party-contributed code is merged? (4) Does the team have documented security awareness training covering social engineering tactics? Specifically: fake job offers, impersonation via professional networks, and requests to install development tools from external sources. (5) Are third-party testing platforms (TestFlight, sideloaded apps) restricted for team members with access to sensitive systems? (6) Are development tool installations (VS Code extensions, npm packages, pip packages) reviewed before use by team members with privileged access? Security Council governance migrated to 2/5 multisig with zero timelock weeks before attack. No contributor vetting policy was documented.
