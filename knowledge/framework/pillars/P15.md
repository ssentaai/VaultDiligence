Principle: a control that is not tested against current data is not a control. This pillar asks whether the risk control operator runs a live loop — scheduled checks at a stated cadence, drift detection against parameter baselines, health of the monitoring itself, a defensible severity ordering, and an escalation apparatus that can act — assessed as the presence or absence of each concrete element, recorded as evidence and never as a summary judgement.
ID | Category | Criterion | What to Measure / How to Verify | Status | Institutional Standard | RF / Critical Conditions | Failure Case Anchor
15.1 | Control Loop | Live Control-Loop Element Coverage (F-CUR-021) | Which live-control elements are present: scheduled monitoring jobs, the stated run cadence, cap-utilisation flag bands, and defined data-freshness SLAs? Record each element as present or absent with evidence — an element inventory recorded as evidence. Cross-reference F-OPS-004 (monitoring dashboard), P8.3 (real-time monitoring), F-LIQ-048 (host-pool utilisation). | Gap with action | The risk control operator runs scheduled checks at a stated cadence against the approved universe and mandates, with cap-utilisation flag bands and freshness SLAs defined; each element is evidenced as present, not asserted. | No scheduled control job, no defined cadence, and no freshness SLA on a managed vault is a critical condition (cross-ref RF15) — controls are not tested against current data. | A monitoring surface that only refreshes on demand cannot catch a parameter or liquidity move between manual looks; the gap between checks is the loss window.
15.2 | Drift Detection | Parameter-Baseline Drift Detection (F-CUR-022) | Does the risk control operator compare current protocol parameters against a stored prior baseline and flag changes in LLTV, supply/borrow caps, oracle fields, collateral lists, and market membership? Confirm the baseline exists and the comparison runs — distinct from merely displaying current values. | Gap with action | Current parameters are diffed against a retained baseline each cycle, and material changes (LLTV, cap, oracle, collateral) are flagged automatically rather than noticed by chance. | No baseline retained and no diff performed is a critical condition — a silent protocol-side parameter change reaches the risk control operator only after it has already repriced risk. | A risk control operator that displays current LLTV but never diffs it against yesterday's learns of a liquidation-repricing change from the liquidation, not before it.
15.3 | Data Health | Data-Health / Monitor-the-Monitor (F-CUR-023) | Does the risk control operator monitor its own monitoring: freshness checks on source data, record-count history, and schema snapshots, with staleness surfaced rather than hidden? Confirm staleness is displayed as a state, not silently suppressed. Cross-reference F-OPS-004 (data lag). | Gap with action | Source-data freshness, record-count continuity, and schema stability are checked and their failures surfaced; a stale feed is shown as stale, so the reader can discount it. | A monitoring layer that cannot detect or does not surface its own staleness is a critical condition — every downstream control silently inherits stale inputs. | A dashboard that shows a confident number computed from a two-day-old feed is more dangerous than one that shows nothing, because it invites reliance.
15.4 | Severity Encoding | Alert-Severity Ordering (F-CUR-024) | Does the risk control operator's alerting encode a defensible severity ordering — an LLTV / liquidation-threshold change treated as the highest severity because it reprices liquidation risk on existing positions, and a supply/borrow cap change treated as lower? Capture the actual mapping as evidence; do not rate it. Cross-reference F-OPS-005 (incident SLA), P2.9 (curator stress record). | Gap with action | The alert model encodes parameter-change severity by real consequence: an LLTV move (reprices existing positions' liquidation risk) ranks above a cap change (bounds new flow), and the mapping is documented. | An alerting model that treats a cap change as more severe than an LLTV change, or that does not classify parameter changes by consequence at all, is a critical condition — the most dangerous change is not the loudest alert. | A severity model inverted so a cap tweak pages the team while an LLTV cut is logged quietly ensures the reprice-existing-positions event is seen last.
15.5 | Escalation Apparatus | Escalation, SLA & Incident Machinery (F-CUR-025) | What escalation apparatus does the risk control operator operate: a defined incident-report SLA window on a breach, a committee with authority to reduce or unwind positions, and incident machinery (paging, war-room / coordinated response)? Confirm the authority to act, not only to notify. Cross-reference F-OPS-005 (incident response SLA), F-OPS-033 (pre-agreed incident communication). | Gap with action | A graded escalation path exists with a stated incident-report window on breaches, a committee holding position-reduction/unwind authority, and named incident-response machinery; escalation ends in action, not just acknowledgement. | An escalation path that can notify but holds no authority to reduce or unwind exposure, or has no stated incident-report window, is a critical condition — detection without authority to act is not a control. | A risk team that can email a breach but cannot force an unwind waits on the same committee whose delay the escalation existed to prevent.
