# P13 · Chain

P13  ·  Chain Risk  ·  Family E: Operational, Infrastructure & Governance
Vault-type applicability note: Applies to every vault — a vault inherits every property of each chain it or its dependency chain is deployed on. For Ethereum-mainnet-only vaults, criteria 13.3-13.5 resolve quickly but are still stated, not skipped: the chain evaluation is the precondition record.
Provenance: tree-native pillar, no xlsx provenance. P13 extends beyond the xlsx-migrated P0-P10. Source: a published protocol risk framework Layer 4 (a published protocol risk framework §4.1-4.10, ARFC stage). Closes the state-of-framework NOT-FOUND family: sequencer decentralisation, reorg/finality treatment, chain-tier propagation. Ingested Sprint 4 per DECISIONS 2026-06-13 (categorical-gap exception). Fields F-CHN-001 through F-CHN-010 (Phase 7.3). Red flag: RF45 (chain critical property below baseline on a material deployment). Scenarios: SC11 (sequencer outage), SC13 (L1 reorg).
Principle: chain risk is a precondition, not a pillar among peers. A vault deployed on a chain inherits sequencer halts, reorgs, governance compromise, and withdrawal lockouts regardless of how clean its own contracts and bridges are. The chain's evaluation therefore sits as an upper bound on every asset-level finding for that deployment (§4.10) — a strong asset on a weak chain is bounded by the chain.

13.1 | Evaluation Gate | Chain Evaluation Gate — Precondition (F-CHN-001) | Confirm a chain-level evaluation exists for every chain in the vault's deployment and dependency set, completed before asset-level assessment. Re-evaluated quarterly and out-of-cycle on material chain events (halt, reorg, governance compromise, major ecosystem participant exit). | Gap with action | Chain evaluation on record for every chain in scope, current within the cadence, predating asset-level conclusions that depend on it. | A vault on a chain with no chain evaluation carries an unassessed precondition — every asset-level finding on that deployment is provisional. State this explicitly. | Asset-level diligence on an unevaluated chain is a house survey on unsurveyed land: the strongest contract findings are bounded by chain properties nobody examined.
13.2 | Architecture | Network Architecture & Security Model (F-CHN-002) | Document consensus mechanism and finality model; settlement layer for L2s (the L2-to-settlement bridge evaluated under P12); VM divergence from standard EVM (precompiles, opcodes, gas model); audits on chain contracts (consensus, settlement, governance) by reputable firms; active bug bounty sized to chain TVL; open-source consensus, execution, and settlement code. | Gap with action | Architecture documented; chain contracts audited; bug bounty active; consensus/settlement code open and publicly auditable. | RF45 · Closed-source consensus or settlement code, missing audits on chain contracts, or material undocumented EVM divergence are critical conditions. | An unaudited settlement contract is asset risk for every asset on the chain simultaneously — chain-level code is the one dependency no asset can diversify away.
13.3 | Decentralisation | Consensus / Sequencer Decentralisation (F-CHN-003) | For L2s: sequencer model (single, shared, permissionless), with the path to permissionless sequencing documented. For L1s: validator count, identity, geographic distribution, entry/exit/slashing mechanics, stake concentration. Both: client diversity (single-client dependency = single point of failure), infrastructure concentration (jurisdiction, cloud, RPC), fee-revenue concentration. | Gap with action | No single-sequencer dependence without documented escape mechanics; validator concentration below honest-majority thresholds; multi-client consensus. | RF45 · Single sequencer with no forced-inclusion path, validator concentration above honest-majority threshold in one entity or jurisdiction, or single-client dependency are critical conditions. Cross-ref SC11. | Arbitrum sequencer outages (Dec 2021, Jun 2022) halted transaction inclusion chain-wide — every position on the chain was frozen for the duration, regardless of asset quality.
13.4 | Finality & Exit | Finality, Withdrawal & Escape Mechanics (F-CHN-004) | Document the finality model and lag (probabilistic / economic / deterministic stated separately, not conflated); reorg history including any reorg deeper than the stated finality assumption; L2 withdrawal mechanism and standing delay; forced-inclusion / escape-hatch availability (absence = sequencer single point of failure); fraud-proof or zk-proof maturity (permissioned or stub proofs = standing constraint); MEV characteristics that affect liquidation execution, including private orderflow concentration. | Gap with action | Bounded reorg depth; documented withdrawal path with stated delay; forced-inclusion or escape hatch present for L2s; live, permissionless proof system. | RF45 · Absent escape hatch, withdrawal mechanics dependent entirely on an honest sequencer, or probabilistic finality without bounded reorg depth are critical conditions. Cross-ref SC11 (sequencer outage) and SC13 (L1 reorg). | Polygon 2022: a reorg deeper than common finality assumptions reorganised more than a hundred blocks. Positions assuming finality at thirty blocks were retroactively rewritten.
13.5 | Chain Governance | Chain Governance & Upgrade Control (F-CHN-005) | Identify upgrade authority for chain contracts (including L2 settlement contracts); multisig composition, quorum, signer identity for upgrade and pause authorities; timelock delays on critical upgrades; pause and emergency-action authorities with invocation conditions; governance token distribution where on-chain governance is the upgrade path; change-management process for hard forks. | Gap with action | Disclosed upgrade authorities with honest-majority control and observation-window timelocks on production-critical upgrades. | RF45 · Single-signer or sub-honest-majority control of chain upgrade authority, or sub-day timelocks on production-critical upgrades, are critical conditions. | A chain upgrade key is a master key to every contract on the chain. Sub-day upgrade paths mean every vault on the chain can be rewritten faster than its depositors can exit.
13.6 | Operational History | Chain Operational History (F-CHN-006) | Compile the chain's incident record: halts (duration, root cause, resolution), reorgs (depth, resolution), published post-mortems, time-to-recovery, recurrence patterns. Evaluate the record against the stated design properties — the record governs where they disagree. | Gap with action | Incident record compiled with post-mortems for material incidents; no unaddressed recurrence pattern. | Repeated halts or reorgs on the same root cause without structural remediation are critical conditions. Cross-ref SC11/SC13. | Solana 2021-2022: repeated chain-wide halts on related root causes. Design documentation promised liveness; the operational record was the binding truth.
13.7 | Ecosystem | Ecosystem Adoption & Market Infrastructure (F-CHN-007) | Document adoption as structural fact, not growth forecast: chain TVL, stablecoin TVL and dominance, active addresses, protocol count and concentration; DeFi vertical coverage (lending, DEXs, stablecoins, derivatives, oracles) with gaps that affect vault operation named; DEX depth on major pairs; bridge accessibility (each stack under P12); on/off-ramp corridors; wallet support; competing lending markets and distorting incentive programmes; crisis-coordination capacity (foundation or community entities able to coordinate liquidity restoration). | Gap with action | Ecosystem supports the primitives the vault depends on; liquidation-relevant DEX depth present; more than one bridge; crisis-coordination capacity identified. | Insufficient DEX depth for liquidation execution, single-bridge dependence, or missing critical oracle infrastructure are critical conditions. | Liquidations that cannot clear at acceptable slippage convert a price event into bad debt for the whole deployment — ecosystem depth is a loss-severity input, not a marketing metric.
13.8 | Tooling | Chain Tooling & Monitoring Support (F-CHN-008) | Confirm the monitoring a vault assessment depends on can actually run: block explorer with verified-source upload and event decoding; indexing/analytics coverage (Dune, DeFiLlama, theGraph or equivalents); RPC provider diversity (single-RPC dependence = binding monitoring constraint); risk-monitoring and oracle providers operating on-chain at required cadence; data availability for asset-state and authority-queue signals. | Gap with action | Full explorer functionality, multi-provider indexing and RPC, oracle and monitoring infrastructure live at required cadence. | A chain that cannot be monitored at the required cadence makes every continuous-monitoring claim for vaults on it unverifiable — state the coverage gap. | Monitoring promises inherit the chain's tooling floor: an alerting SLA on a chain with one RPC provider and no indexer is a promise without infrastructure.
13.9 | Base Liquidity | Native & Major-Asset Liquidity (F-CHN-009) | Document the chain's structural liquidity backbone: native token DEX volume distribution, price stability, collateralisation rates; availability and depth of major assets (ETH-correlated, BTC-correlated, stablecoins); organic versus incentive-driven volume decomposition. | Gap with action | Deep, stable, organically-supported liquidity in the chain's native and major assets. | Native-token instability, thin LP diversity on the chain's deepest pools, or volume materially supported by incentive programmes are critical conditions — they propagate to every listing's exit depth. | When incentives end, incentive-driven depth leaves first — exactly when exits need it. Base-asset liquidity is the floor under every other asset's liquidation path.
13.10 | Propagation | Chain-Risk Propagation to Asset Exposure (F-CHN-010) | State explicitly how the chain evaluation bounds the vault: the chain's standing constraints apply to every asset on the deployment regardless of asset-level findings; cross-chain expansion onto a weaker chain carries that chain's constraints onto the bridged asset; material chain-risk degradation without remediation is a deprecation-class event for the deployment. | Gap with action | Chain-tier-as-upper-bound stated in the assessment output for each deployment; bridged-asset exposure carries the weaker chain's constraints. | A pack that reports asset-level findings without stating the chain-level bound overstates precision — the bound is mandatory output, not optional context. | An asset evaluated clean on its own terms, deployed on a chain with a single sequencer and no escape hatch, is exactly as exitable as that sequencer is honest. The chain bound is the binding one.
