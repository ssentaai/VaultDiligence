# P1 · Legal

P1  ·  Legal Structure & Claim Enforceability  ·  Family A: Capital Safety
Vault-type applicability note: Most material for VT-3 (RWA), VT-4 (Structured Finance). Applies universally.
ID | Category | Criterion | What to Measure / How to Verify | Status | Institutional Standard | RF / Critical Conditions | Failure Case Anchor
1.1 | Legal Isolation | Asset Ring-Fencing from Operator Insolvency | Can deposited assets be claimed by operator's creditors in bankruptcy? Test: would a Cayman liquidator have access to client assets? | Gap with action | Assets held in trust, SPV, or foundation legally isolated from operator balance sheet. Tier-1 legal opinion confirms bankruptcy remoteness. | RF25 · No legal isolation; assets could be claimed by operator creditors; no trust or foundation structure | Celsius, BlockFi: client assets on operator balance sheet — lost in insolvency
1.2 | Beneficial Ownership | Depositor Beneficial Ownership Preserved Throughout | Does depositor retain beneficial ownership of underlying assets (BTC, ETH, USDC) at all times, or is ownership transferred to the protocol? | Gap with action | Beneficial ownership retained by depositor. Legal opinion confirms. Tokens represent claim on underlying, not protocol equity. | Beneficial ownership transferred to protocol on deposit; depositor receives only protocol token with no underlying claim | FTX: customer funds re-characterised as unsecured claims in bankruptcy; beneficial ownership untested until too late
1.3 | Claim Enforceability | Senior / Principal Claim is Court-Enforceable | Is the principal claim enforceable in a court of competent jurisdiction independent of smart contract operation? What jurisdiction? What law governs? | Gap with action | Written legal opinion from Tier-1 firm in issuer jurisdiction confirming claim is court-enforceable. English, NY, or Cayman law preferred. | RF09, RF25 · No legal opinion; claim exists only in code; no governing law specified; claim unenforceable if smart contract fails | Multiple DeFi protocols: 'code is law' meant no recourse when code failed or was exploited
1.4 | Structure Quality | Legal Entity, Jurisdiction & Substance | What is the legal entity type? Jurisdiction? Does it meet economic substance requirements? Is there an independent director or trustee? | Gap with action | Recognised jurisdiction (Cayman, BVI, Luxembourg, Delaware). Substance requirements met. Independent director or trustee appointed. | RF27 · No legal entity; jurisdiction shopping without substance; anonymous entity; no independent oversight | Multiple structured products collapsed because SPV had no substance and operator could raid it
1.5 | Documentation | Master Agreement, T&Cs & Dispute Resolution | Is there a signed master agreement? What are depositor rights? What dispute resolution mechanism exists? Can terms be changed unilaterally? | Gap with action | Signed master agreement; governing law clause; arbitration or court jurisdiction; 30-day notice for material term changes; depositor consent required. | No signed agreement; terms changeable without notice; no dispute resolution; oral terms only | Retail crypto platforms changed T&Cs mid-crisis to block withdrawals — no legal agreement meant no recourse
1.6 | Wind-Down | Orderly Wind-Down & Recovery Mechanism | If the protocol shuts down (regulatory, voluntary, or insolvency), how do depositors recover assets? Timeline? Costs? Independent trustee? | Gap with action | Documented wind-down procedure. Depositors recover underlying assets (not just protocol tokens) within stated timeline. Independent trustee can act. | RF21 · No wind-down plan; underlying assets unrecoverable without operator cooperation; no independent trustee | Celsius wind-down took 18+ months with 30%+ haircut due to absence of any documented recovery mechanism
