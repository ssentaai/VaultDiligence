This pillar targets the Risk Control Operator role (F-ROL-001, P17 Counterparty & Role Registry); the criteria fields retain their F-CUR IDs under the F-CUR prefix for continuity.
Principle: a risk control operator that publishes what it does not yet cover is more trustworthy than one that implies full coverage — and the location of its gaps matters as much as their count. This pillar asks whether the risk control operator discloses a requirement-level coverage map, where the disclosed gaps fall relative to tail risk, and what risk signals it actually computes and runs on its own book. Coverage and signals are recorded as evidence, never as a summary judgement of the risk control operator.
ID | Category | Criterion | What to Measure / How to Verify | Status | Institutional Standard | RF / Critical Conditions | Failure Case Anchor
16.1 | Coverage Disclosure | Requirement-Level Coverage Map Published (F-CUR-026) | Does the risk control operator publish a requirement-level map of its own framework against its monitoring implementation, marking each requirement full / partial / not-covered? Confirm the map exists and is requirement-level, not a single headline figure. Record its presence and the full/partial/none counts as evidence. | Gap with action | The risk control operator publishes a requirement-by-requirement coverage map (full / partial / none) of its framework vs what it actually monitors; a risk control operator that can produce one is disclosing its own blind spots. | A risk control operator that cannot produce a requirement-level coverage map, or discloses only a single headline coverage figure with no per-requirement breakdown, is a transparency gap — its blind spots are unstated. | A risk control operator that implies full coverage but has never mapped requirement to implementation does not itself know which requirements it silently fails to monitor.
16.2 | Coverage Gap Profile | Where Coverage Gaps Fall (F-CUR-027) | Given a coverage map (F-CUR-026), where do the not-covered and partial requirements fall — concentrated in tail-risk categories (correlation, liquidity) that drive drawdowns in systemic stress, or in easily-instrumented categories (leverage)? Record the gap distribution by category as evidence. Cross-reference F-CUR-026. | Gap with action | The disclosed coverage gaps are distributed away from the tail-risk categories; where gaps concentrate in correlation and liquidity, that concentration is stated plainly so the allocator sees where monitoring is thinnest exactly where stress bites. | Coverage concentrated in easily-instrumented categories while correlation and liquidity are largely uncovered is a critical condition for stress resilience — the strong coverage is where it is easiest, not where it matters most. | A risk control operator scoring high on leverage coverage and near-zero on correlation is well-instrumented for the risk that rarely causes the loss and blind to the one that does.
16.3 | Signals | Risk Signals Computed & Own-Book Use (F-CUR-028) | Which risk signals does the risk control operator compute, at what cadence, and at what level (protocol solvency, vault utilisation/collateral, market liquidity/exit-cost/whale-concentration, anomaly detection), and does it run those same signals on its own book? Record the signal inventory and cadence as evidence. Cross-reference F-OPS-030 (quantitative risk reporting). | Gap with action | The risk control operator computes named risk signals at a stated cadence across protocol, vault, and market levels, and applies the same signals to its own positions — alignment between what it measures for clients and for itself. | No computed risk signals beyond static reporting, or signals marketed but not run on the risk control operator's own book, is a gap in both capability and alignment. | A risk control operator that publishes signals it does not use on its own capital is selling a tool it does not trust; the misalignment is the finding.
