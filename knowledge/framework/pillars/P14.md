Principle: a vault's risk is inseparable from the competence and discipline of the risk control operator that controls it. This pillar asks whether the risk control operator runs a structured diligence apparatus, knows and documents where parameter authority sits at each venue, exercises any control it holds through policy rather than discretion, and separates execution from oversight with institutional key custody.
ID | Category | Criterion | What to Measure / How to Verify | Status | Institutional Standard | RF / Critical Conditions | Failure Case Anchor
14.1 | Diligence Apparatus | Structured DDQ Process (F-CUR-017) | Does the risk control operator run a structured, documented due-diligence process before a protocol or asset enters its approved universe: one dossier per protocol/asset, numbered review cycles, defined evidence standards and accepted formats, and explicit decision gates (approve / conditional / reject / defer / re-review)? Confirm the process exists as artifacts, not as a claim. | Gap with action | The risk control operator operates a documented DDQ apparatus with per-candidate dossiers, numbered review cycles, stated evidence standards, and named decision states; a candidate is a review object before it can be deployed to. | Ad-hoc onboarding with no documented dossier, review-cycle, or decision-gate record is a critical condition for a managed vault — the approved universe cannot be reconstructed or audited. | A risk control operator that onboards by informal judgement leaves no reconstructable record of why an asset was approved; the first disputed listing has no evidentiary trail.
14.2 | Parameter Authority | Venue Parameter-Authority Split (F-CUR-018) | For every venue the risk control operator operates on, which risk parameters (supply caps, borrow caps, LLTV / liquidation threshold, oracle route, market configuration) are protocol- or governance-controlled versus directly curator-controlled? Classify each venue {protocol-governed, curator-governed, hybrid} and name the per-parameter owner. Cross-reference F-CTR-007 (parameter change authority), F-CUR-008 (parameter change log), P2.8 (automated allocation). | Gap with action | Each venue is classified by where parameter authority sits, per parameter, so it is unambiguous whether curator competence or protocol governance is the binding control on any given setting (Aave/Kamino protocol-governed vs Euler/Morpho curator-governed). | Undisclosed or unknown parameter authority on a venue carrying material exposure is a critical condition — the allocator cannot tell whether the risk control operator can act or only lobby. | A risk control operator assumed to control a cap it can only petition the protocol to change cannot execute a stress response it implicitly promised.
14.3 | Parameter Discipline | Curator Control Exercised via Policy (F-CUR-019) | Where the risk control operator holds direct parameter control (curator-governed venues per F-CUR-018), is that control exercised through a documented internal policy — approved models behind LLTV / cap settings, a change log, and a review/approval step (e.g. four-eyes) — rather than unconstrained discretion? Cross-reference F-CUR-018, F-CUR-008. | Gap with action | Direct curator control is governed by documented policy: model-vetting before a value is set, a parameter change log, and an approval step separating the analyst who proposes from the party who commits. | Direct curator control with no documented policy, model vetting, or change log is a critical condition — parameters can move on undocumented discretion. | Unpoliced curator control is the Euler/Morpho failure surface: the curator can set a parameter wrong as easily as right, with nothing between intent and effect.
14.4 | Execution & Custody | Execution/Oversight Separation & Key Custody (F-CUR-020) | Is execution separated from risk oversight, and what governs the execution surface? Capture: executor type (application / on-chain bot / keeper / manual), signer architecture, permission scopes per action, timelock, kill-switch authority, and change-management on any execution agent or bot. Cross-reference F-CTR-004 (timelock), F-CTR-015 (custody chain), F-CTR-016 (guardian scope), F-CTR-027 (per-action timelock), F-CTR-028 (multisig threshold), F-OPS-011 (signer identity). | Gap with action | Execution is documented as separate from oversight, with institutional key custody, scoped permissions, timelocks, a kill switch, and change-management on execution logic; where bots execute outside the risk/monitoring layer, that boundary and its controls are explicit. | Execution agents or bots that act outside the risk-oversight layer with undisclosed signer architecture, permission scopes, or change-management are a critical condition (cross-ref RF03, RF42). | If bots execute outside the monitoring layer and the signer/kill-switch model is unknown, the risk control operator's oversight is a dashboard, not a control — the highest-severity open question for a managed vault.
