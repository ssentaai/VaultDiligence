# P12 · Bridging

P12  ·  Bridging Risk  ·  Family A: Capital Safety
Vault-type applicability note: Applies to any vault whose token, collateral, or dependency chain crosses a bridge or cross-chain messaging system. N/A for single-chain vaults with no bridged collateral and no cross-chain dependency — state why explicitly.
Provenance: tree-native pillar, no xlsx provenance. P12 extends beyond the xlsx-migrated P0-P10. Source: a published protocol risk framework Layer 2 (a published protocol risk framework §2.1-2.16, ARFC stage), grounded by the Kelp DAO April 2026 1-of-1 DVN exploit ($292M) and the synthetic-dollar issuer/a tokenized CLO fund applied-diligence finding that bridge risk could not be quantified because adapter implementation and quorum configuration were undisclosed. Ingested Sprint 4 per DECISIONS 2026-06-13 (categorical-gap exception). Fields F-BRG-001 through F-BRG-013 (Phase 7.3). Red flag: RF44 (bridge configuration below mandatory baseline on a material route). Scenario: SC12 (bridge pause).
Principle: the bridge configuration travels with the asset. A vault's bridging risk is the risk of its weakest route, and the weaker side of any two custody or authority arrangements governs the surface. The framework evaluates routes vendor-agnostically — vendor-managed defaults below the baseline are findings, not mitigations.

12.1 | Route Topology | Bridge Route Topology Disclosure (F-BRG-001) | Document every route at route level before assessment: origin chain and canonical supply; target-chain representation (mint-and-burn, lock-and-mint, native-bridge); bridge or messaging system by name and version; verifier/attestor/oracle configuration per route; bridge admin and upgrade controls on both chains. Weak origin-chain controls are assessed as part of the target-chain exposure. Topology disclosure is the precondition for every other criterion in this pillar. | Gap with action | Complete route-level topology disclosed for every route carrying vault exposure. No undocumented route. | RF44 · Incomplete topology disclosure on any route carrying material exposure is a critical condition — the remaining P12 criteria cannot be assessed without it. | a synthetic-dollar and CLO-fund diligence 2026: bridge security risk could not be quantified — adapter implementation and quorum configuration undisclosed. The gap blocked a real allocator's assessment.
12.2 | Verifier Threshold | Verifier-Set Threshold — Minimum Three Independent (F-BRG-002) | Count verifiers as independent operator/trust domains: a verifier network run by one organisation counts as one unit regardless of internal node count. Confirm at least three independent verifiers (validators, attestors, nodes, or message verifiers) on every route carrying vault exposure. Verify on-chain, not from vendor documentation alone. | Gap with action | Minimum three independent verifiers on every route. One-of-N and two-of-N configurations are below baseline regardless of vendor. | RF44 · Any route at one-of-N or two-of-N verification is a critical condition. Record whether the configuration is vendor-default or operator-chosen — known-weak defaults are a finding in both cases. | Kelp DAO April 2026: 1-of-1 LayerZero DVN forced-failover, $292M drained. LayerZero shipped 1-of-1 as default config for a large share of integrators.
12.3 | Verifier Independence | Verifier Independence — Organisation, Jurisdiction, Infrastructure (F-BRG-003) | Test the nominal verifier count against three independence dimensions: (1) organisational — distinct operators, no economic co-dependence; (2) jurisdictional — concentration under a single regulatory action; (3) infrastructure — shared RPC providers, cloud accounts, hardware, or key infrastructure. Document identifiable overlap. | Gap with action | Verifiers independent across organisation, jurisdiction, and infrastructure. Bare-metal or comparably isolated infrastructure preferred. | Nominal verifier-set decentralisation that collapses on any independence dimension is a single point of failure — state it as such. | A three-of-five verifier set sharing one cloud provider or one RPC vendor is one verifier under stress. Supply-chain concentration converts redundancy to theatre.
12.4 | Library Pinning | Send/Receive Library Pinning (F-BRG-004) | Confirm receive libraries (or vendor equivalent) are pinned so vendor-side compromise cannot silently change the verifier set or message-handling logic. Library upgrades require issuer explicit re-attestation, not a vendor-controlled upgrade path. Verifier-set changes gated by bridge-authority timelocks on every chain. Pinned configuration documented in route topology. | Gap with action | Libraries pinned; upgrades require issuer re-attestation; pinned configuration documented. | Unpinned receive libraries on routes carrying vault exposure are a critical condition until pinning is in place. | Vendor-side authority compromise can rewire verification without the issuer ever signing — the attack needs no exploit of the issuer at all.
12.5 | Authority Timelocks | Bridge Authority & Ownership Timelocks (F-BRG-005) | Enumerate bridge authorities: ownership transfer, verifier-set change, library upgrade, rate-limit change, mint/burn authority grant. Confirm a timelock gates each, on every route, with delay sufficient for mitigating action before execution. Instantaneous or sub-hour delays are below baseline. Cross-reference F-CTR-016 (pause authority) — this criterion extends it to bridge-level authority. | Gap with action | Timelocks on all five bridge authority classes, every route, with observation-window delay. | RF44 · Absence of a timelock on any bridge authority is a critical condition. | Drift April 2026: governance migration path carried zero timelock weeks before the $285M exploit — authority-without-observation-window is the same control failure class at bridge level.
12.6 | Pause Pathways | Independent Pause Pathways (F-BRG-006) | Map every pause path on the route: issuer-side pause independent of the vendor (including native rate limits set to zero), vendor-side verification halt exercisable by its incident-response team, and any protocol-controllable pause via the monitoring layer. Confirm each path is documented and tested under standing incident-response procedures. | Gap with action | At least two independent pause pathways, each documented and tested. Single-path pause concentration is below baseline. | Reliance on a single pause path concentrates incident-response authority in one party — state the single point of failure. Cross-ref SC12 (bridge pause scenario): a pause is also an exit-freeze for holders. | Kelp April 2026: a pauser froze the bridge 46 minutes post-drain, blocking roughly $200M of follow-on theft. Pause latency and pause authority decided the loss boundary.
12.7 | Bridge-Layer Custody | Custody of Bridge Authorities — Both Sides (F-BRG-007) | Identify every authority that can affect vault-relevant bridged assets (mint, burn, pause, rate-limit change) on the bridge-stack side and the issuer side. Confirm institutional-grade key custody on both. The weaker side governs the surface. Custody arrangements disclosed; custody changes disclosed in advance as material changes. Cross-reference F-CTR-015 (custody enumeration) — this extends it to the bridge layer. | Gap with action | Institutional-grade key custody on both bridge-stack and issuer authorities, disclosed and current. | Weak custody on either side, or undisclosed arrangements, is a critical condition — the weaker side sets the exposure. | Ronin March 2022: five of nine validator keys compromised, $625M. Multichain July 2023: CEO-held keys, $126M gone with one person. Bridge custody failures are total-loss events.
12.8 | Rate Limiting | Per-Route Rate Limiting (F-BRG-008) | Confirm rate limiting is present and enforced at the bridge stack or app level as a standing property, not a stress contingency. Where native rate limiting is unavailable, external layering must have equivalent enforceability. Limits configured per route, inbound and outbound sized separately. Configurations documented under route topology and publicly cross-referenced in vendor documentation where possible. | Gap with action | Per-route, per-direction rate limits enforced and documented on every route carrying vault exposure. Native limits publicly documented by the vendor preferred. | RF44 · Routes with material exposure and no effective rate limit are a critical condition — the worst-case single-transaction drain is unbounded. | Wormhole February 2022: a single forged message minted 120K wETH ($326M) in one transaction. A binding per-route limit bounds exactly this class of loss.
12.9 | Rate-Limit Sizing | Rate-Limit Sizing — Highest Sustained Flow (F-BRG-009) | Evaluate the limit values, not just their existence: sized to highest observed sustained flow per route (not peak burst); headroom over sustained flow explicit and bounded; inbound and outbound evaluated separately (cross-chain flow is typically asymmetric). Depends on F-BRG-008 being present. | Gap with action | Limits sized to highest sustained flow with explicit bounded headroom, per direction. | Unbounded headroom is the absence of a meaningful limit — treat as F-BRG-008 fail. | A limit sized to ten times any flow the route has ever carried binds nothing: a forged package drains to the limit, and the limit was set by optimism.
12.10 | Incident Response | Bridge Incident Response & Monitoring Coverage (F-BRG-010) | Confirm standing 24/7 reachability across every party with authority to mitigate an in-flight incident: vendor, attestors, issuer. Written exploit procedure documented and rehearsed. Pre-agreed authority for on-call teams to pause contracts and lower rate limits without escalation (defensive tightening may bypass timelocks; loosening may not). Dedicated security and monitoring teams on both vendor and issuer side with documented coverage — monitoring offloaded entirely to consuming protocols is below baseline. | Gap with action | 24/7 reachability across all parties; rehearsed written procedure; pre-agreed pause authority; dedicated monitoring teams on both sides. | Gaps in reachability across any party in the chain, or absent dedicated monitoring on either side, are critical conditions. Cross-ref SC12. | Kelp April 2026: the 46-minute detection-to-pause window was the difference between $292M and roughly $492M. Incident response speed is a quantifiable control.
12.11 | Configuration Lifecycle | Bridge Configuration Lifecycle — Initial / Review / Cadence / Decayed Lanes (F-BRG-011) | Confirm the configuration lifecycle is governed end-to-end: (1) initial per-lane limits proposed by the issuer at integration, documented per route and direction; (2) independent review completed before the route goes live, divergences documented; (3) re-evaluation at quarterly cadence and out-of-cycle on triggers (new chain deployment, new liquidity venue, sustained transit-volume shift, material authority/verifier/limit change); (4) decayed lanes closed rather than left at elevated limits — every open lane is standing attack surface independent of traffic. | Gap with action | Documented lifecycle covering initial limits, pre-launch review, quarterly + out-of-cycle re-evaluation, and decayed-lane closure under the same timelocked authority as rate-limit changes. | Routes live without completed review, or decayed lanes left open at stale limits, are critical conditions. | A lane opened for a launch campaign and forgotten at its launch limits is an unwatched door: the attack surface persists after the traffic that justified it is gone.
12.12 | Stack Infrastructure Risk | Bridge-Stack Infrastructure-Risk Evaluation (F-BRG-012) | For each cross-chain stack in the dependency chain, assess: vendor and attestor operational security; code quality and audit reports for on-chain and off-chain components; novel architectural attack surfaces unique to the design; exploit history including recurrence and remediation quality; and the standing monitoring effort the stack demands. | Gap with action | Each stack assessed on all five dimensions with sources cited. Stacks whose monitoring cost is disproportionate to the exposure they secure are findings. | Un-assessed stacks in the dependency chain are gaps, not assumed-safe defaults. | Every bridge stack added to a dependency chain is an independent failure vector — compounded, not averaged, across the chain (cf. F-CTR-019 full bridge enumeration).
12.13 | Configuration Uniformity | Bridge-Config Uniformity vs Per-Chain Flexibility (F-BRG-013) | Determine whether the asset uses a uniform default security architecture across all chain expansions or bespoke per-chain configurations. Bespoke configurations must be explicitly justified and disclosed under route topology. Uniformity reviewed at every cadence point. | Gap with action | Uniform default architecture across expansions, or bespoke deviations explicitly justified and disclosed. | Undisclosed per-chain configuration divergence is a critical condition — it voids the assumption that assessing one route assesses them all. | A bespoke route configured below the asset's own default is invisible to anyone who assessed only the flagship route. Configuration drift is where uniform-sounding security claims quietly fail.
