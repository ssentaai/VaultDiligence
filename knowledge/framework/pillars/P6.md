6.7 | Yield Obligation Type | Discretionary vs Contractual Yield Obligation | Is the underlying yield obligation contractual or discretionary? Contractual: bond coupon, loan interest, fixed RWA coupon — issuer breaches if not paid. Discretionary: preferred equity dividend, at-the-money issuance income, governance reward — issuer can suspend without breach. WORKED PRECEDENT: a listed preferred instrument pays an 11.5% annualised dividend on the issuer's perpetual preferred equity. Per the issuer's own legal disclosure: dividends are formally discretionary. the issuer has strong incentive to maintain (2.25B cash reserve, 28.5 months coverage) but suspension is legally permissible without default. Additionally: per the issuer's February 2026 SEC disclosure, 100% of 2025 the listed preferred instrument's distributions were classified as return of capital for US federal income tax purposes — not income. A vault promising 11.5% yield backed by a discretionary dividend that is legally return of capital must disclose both facts explicitly. Verify: issuer prospectus / preferred stock terms / SEC filings / legal opinion on dividend character. | Gap with action | Contractual yield obligation with no suspension right. Or: discretionary obligation with explicit written disclosure to depositors, cash reserve coverage quantified, and risk clearly stated. | RF43 · Yield is discretionary with no disclosure to depositors; yield classified as return of capital with no tax disclosure; no cash reserve coverage analysis for discretionary obligations | the issuer's listed preferred instrument: dividends formally discretionary. 100% classified as return of capital in 2025. A vault backed solely by a listed preferred instrument distributing yield as 'income' without this disclosure creates material misrepresentation risk.
6.8 | Yield Concentration | Single Underlying Concentration in Yield Source | Is the vault's yield 100% dependent on a single named issuer, instrument, or counterparty? What is the exit scenario if that issuer suspends payment, the instrument depegs, or the counterparty defaults? WORKED PRECEDENT: a preferred-equity-backed token is 100% backed by a listed preferred instrument at launch. If the issuer suspends the listed preferred instrument dividend, or if a listed preferred instrument trades materially below $100 par, a preferred-equity-backed token's entire yield thesis breaks. There is no secondary yield source. The RFC discloses an adaptive LTV model that shifts toward treasuries at high LTV, but this provides yield reduction not yield replacement — a preferred-equity-backed token yield compresses to T-bill rate in stress. Mandatory assessment: (1) Name the single underlying. (2) What % of yield comes from it. (3) What is the exit scenario if it fails. (4) What is the secondary yield floor. (5) Does the vault documentation disclose the concentration explicitly. Sources: vault documentation, RFC/governance forum post, issuer investor relations. | Gap with action | No single issuer >70% of yield. Or: concentration disclosed explicitly with exit scenario and secondary floor stated. Concentration risk understood and accepted by allocator in writing. | RF43 · 100% yield from single discretionary source with no secondary floor; concentration not disclosed to depositors; no exit scenario modelled | a preferred-equity-backed token April 2026: 100% backed by a listed preferred instrument at launch. Yield floor in stress = T-bill rate (~3.5%). Allocator must explicitly accept this concentration. Anchor Protocol precedent: 20% APY 100% from UST reserve subsidy — undisclosed concentration, collapsed in days.
