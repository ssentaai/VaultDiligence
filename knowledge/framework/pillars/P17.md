# P17 · Counterparty & Role Registry

P17  ·  Counterparty & Role Registry  ·  Family E: Operational, Infrastructure & Governance
Vault-type applicability note: Every role is asked for every vault. Individual roles resolve N/A by vault type — state why (e.g. Bridge/Messaging N/A for a single-chain vault; Sub-Advisor N/A where none is appointed; Prime Broker N/A for a fully on-chain vault). A role that exists in the structure but is unnamed or undisclosed is a finding (G2/G3), never a silent absence.
Provenance: tree-native pillar, no xlsx provenance. P17 extends beyond the xlsx-migrated P0-P10 (like P12/P13). Built 2026-07-08 to name every party in a tokenised vault's stack as its own identified-or-gapped field, disentangling roles previously conflated (Risk Control Operator vs Curator vs Investment Manager vs Security Monitor). See an internal analysis. Fields F-ROL-001..020.
Principle: an allocator cannot assess a counterparty it cannot name. This pillar identifies every role in the vault's stack — principal, service provider, infrastructure, and capital/governance — as a discrete field: name the party (evidenced), record the role as gapped (a finding), or mark N/A for the vault type with a stated reason. Detail for a role (custody terms, audit scope, oracle configuration) lives in its own pillar and is cross-referenced by ID, never duplicated here.

17.2 | Service Providers | Service Providers Named (F-ROL-007..014) | Name each service-provider role in use: Custodian (F-ROL-007), Crypto Custodian (F-ROL-008), Administrator/NAV Agent (F-ROL-009), Auditor (F-ROL-010), Transfer Agent (F-ROL-011), Paying Agent (F-ROL-012), Prime Broker (F-ROL-013), Legal Counsel (F-ROL-014). | Gap with action | Every service-provider role in use is named to a verifiable entity; detail such as custody terms or audit scope is cross-referenced to its own field, not restated here. | An unnamed service provider at a critical layer (custody, administration, audit) is a critical condition — cross-ref P0.2. | Celsius/FTX: an unnamed or self-dealing custodian/administrator was the layer where client assets were lost; naming the party is the first control.
17.3 | Infrastructure | Infrastructure Providers Named (F-ROL-015..018) | Name each infrastructure provider the vault depends on: Oracle Provider (F-ROL-015), Bridge/Messaging Provider (F-ROL-016), Security Monitor (F-ROL-017), RPC/Data Provider (F-ROL-018). | Gap with action | Every infrastructure dependency is named, and the Security Monitor is identified distinctly from the Risk Control Operator (the two were previously conflated in one field). | An unnamed infrastructure dependency (oracle, bridge, RPC) carrying material exposure is a critical condition. | Kelp/LayerZero: the bridge/messaging provider was the failure point; a vault that cannot name its bridge or RPC provider cannot assess the dependency that can freeze it.
17.4 | Capital & Governance | Capital & Governance Parties Named (F-ROL-019..020) | Name the Seed/Anchor Allocator (F-ROL-019) and the Governance Body — DAO / Risk Committee / Board (F-ROL-020). | Gap with action | The seed/anchor allocator and the governance body are named; concentration and governance detail cross-reference their own fields (F-FIN-084, F-GOV-022). | An undisclosed anchor allocator on a concentrated vault, or an unnamed governance body where on-chain governance controls parameters, is a critical condition. | a tokenized CLO fund: the seed/anchor allocator (a seed/anchor allocator) was also a top holder; naming the role surfaces the concentration and second-order exposure a holder list alone hides.
