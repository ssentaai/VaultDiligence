10.6 | Underlying Tax Character | Underlying Instrument Tax Character & Depositor Tax Consequence | What is the tax character of the underlying yield instrument the vault is backed by? This is distinct from the vault token's own tax treatment (P10.5). It concerns what the yield received by depositors actually represents in tax terms. Mandatory questions: (1) Is the underlying yield source a contractual payment (interest, rent, coupon — ordinary income) or a discretionary distribution (preferred dividend, return of capital)? (2) If a preferred equity dividend: has the issuer made any return of capital (ROC) classification? ROC distributions reduce the investor's cost basis rather than being treated as income. The effective tax rate is deferred capital gain, not ordinary income — materially different for most institutional allocators. (3) For non-US underlying instruments: what withholding tax applies? (4) Has the vault operator obtained tax counsel opinion on how the underlying instrument's distributions flow through to vault token holders? WORKED PRECEDENT: the issuer announced February 2, 2026 that 100% of 2025 the listed preferred instrument's distributions were classified as return of capital for US federal income tax purposes per SEC Form 8937. A US allocator in a preferred-equity-backed token receiving 11.5% headline yield is therefore receiving ROC, not income, which means the effective tax consequence is deferred capital gain on basis reduction — not annual ordinary income tax. This changes net effective yield materially for taxable allocators.
