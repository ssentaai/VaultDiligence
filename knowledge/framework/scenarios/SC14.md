# SC14 — Third-party leveraged-holder insolvency → redemption-queue pressure with NAV at par

## Scenario
A large holder of the vault token has financed its position with external leverage (borrowed against the token on a lending market, or held it inside a looped strategy). That holder becomes insolvent or is liquidated for reasons unrelated to the vault's own collateral. The forced unwind dumps the token into its exit pool and floods the redemption queue — even though the vault's NAV is at par and nothing about the underlying has changed. A solvent vault faces a run driven entirely by a third party's balance sheet.

## Trigger conditions (observable)
- A third-party leveraged position against the token is large relative to the token's exit-pool depth.
- That position is liquidated or the holder enters insolvency.
- Redemption-queue inflow spikes while NAV remains at par — the pressure is second-order, not credit-driven.

## What to assess (evidence only)
1. Is there a register of third-party leverage against the token, refreshed at a stated cadence? (F-FIN-085)
2. What is the host-pool utilisation and illiquidity threshold on the token's primary exit venue? (F-LIQ-048)
3. What is the external leverage multiplier and the primary correlated exit pool's depth? (F-FIN-061, F-FIN-062, F-FIN-063)
4. What is the redemption capacity and settlement distribution under a queue spike at par? (F-LIQ-046, F-LIQ-047)
5. Does the loss waterfall and run model distinguish credit-driven outflow from second-order forced-unwind outflow? (F-FIN-082, P9.9)

## Documented response expected
A response naming the largest third-party leveraged holders, the exit-pool depth against their position size, and the redemption-queue behaviour when a par-NAV token faces a forced external unwind. If none exists: state the gap with a precise action (request the third-party-leverage register and exit-pool depth).
